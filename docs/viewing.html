Viewing and interactions

Choose how molecules are drawn, colored and lit, focus a residue or ligand to see how it binds, measure distances and angles, and work with biological assemblies and ensembles.

Representations

The Representation presets in the Style tab set the polymer and ligand styles together. Press 1 cartoon, 2 ball and stick, 3 sticks, 4 spacefill, 5 trace or 6 surface, or run a command such as preset sticks. You can also set each component separately:

ComponentOptions
PolymerCartoon, trace, ball and stick, sticks, spacefill or hidden. The cartoon draws helices as ribbons, strands as flat arrows and loops as tubes, with smooth transitions. Nucleic acids get a phosphate-backbone tube with base slabs.
Ligands and ionsBall and stick, sticks, spacefill or hidden. Ions are drawn as spheres, and metal-coordination bonds are dashed.
Side chainsAround focus (the default) shows side chains within 5 Å of the focused residue or ligand. All shows every side chain on top of the cartoon.
Water and hydrogensToggles, also on the W and H keys.
SurfaceFour surface types with their own coloring and opacity; see Surfaces.

With several structures in the scene, changes in the Style tab apply to all of them or only to the active one.

Modified residues such as selenomethionine and phosphoserine stay part of the polymer. Unknown residues with a linked peptide or sugar-phosphate backbone are recognized automatically.

Surfaces

  • Types: Molecular (SES), Solvent accessible (SAS), Gaussian or van der Waals.
  • Coloring can match the color scheme, or show Coulombic electrostatics or hydrophobicity.
  • Opacity is adjustable; a transparent surface shows the cartoon underneath.
  • Surfaces are computed in a background worker, taking about 0.2 s for a 5,000-atom protein.

Color schemes

Pick a color scheme in the Style tab. The legend in the view describes the active scheme and the surface coloring; click a legend's title to collapse it. The color command applies a scheme by name, for example color chain or color plddt. Schemes apply to whole structures; to color part of one, use a named color or hex value with a selection, as in color magenta /A:315 (see Selections and commands).

Lighting and effects

Lighting presets set the overall look, from standard shading to flat, outlined figures. Choose one in the Style tab, or run a command such as lighting illustrative.

PresetLook
StandardKey light plus headlamp, ambient occlusion, depth fog.
SoftStrong ambient occlusion with little direct light, similar to ChimeraX "soft".
IllustrativeFlat colors, black outlines and ambient occlusion, for Goodsell-style figures.
GlossyStrong specular highlights.
NeonAdditive glow on a dark background (the original Proteoscope look).
FlatUnlit colors with outlines.

Beyond the presets, you can fine-tune the look:

  • Sliders for ambient occlusion strength and radius, outline, depth fog, glow and specular.
  • Backgrounds: dark, black, gray or white, also set with bg white and the like.
  • Front clip and Back clip cut slabs through the molecule without rebuilding geometry; clipped atoms are capped.
  • O switches between orthographic and perspective projection.

Selection, focus and interactions

Click to select a residue; double-click to focus it. Focusing a residue or ligand frames its binding site and draws its non-covalent interactions.

Selecting

Click an atom to select its residue, and Shift-click to add more; clicking another structure makes it active. In the sequence panel, click to select, shift-click to add and drag for a range. Esc clears the selection. The Selection card in the right panel shows:

  • the residue type, including modifications;
  • the secondary structure and its source, including the DSSP code;
  • the φ/ψ angles;
  • the B-factor or pLDDT;
  • the UniProt position;
  • relative SASA and proteomics values, when computed.

Sticks, Color, Hide and Reset on the Selection card apply to the selected residues, and All in the Chains card shows hidden residues again. To select by a rule, such as everything within 5 Å of a ligand, see Selections and commands.

Focusing

Double-click a residue or ligand, or select one and press F, to focus it. The focus command does the same for a selection, for example focus resn HEM and chain A. When you focus:

  • the camera frames the binding site;
  • side chains within 5 Å appear;
  • non-covalent interactions are detected and drawn as colored dashed lines, and listed in the right panel.

Esc clears the focus.

Interaction types

The criteria follow PLIP (Salentin et al. 2015). Proteoscope detects:

  • hydrogen bonds;
  • salt bridges;
  • π-stacking, parallel and T-shaped;
  • cation-π interactions;
  • hydrophobic contacts;
  • halogen bonds;
  • metal coordination;
  • water bridges, when water is shown.

Each type can be toggled in the Analysis tab. Analyze interface lists the contacts between two chains (the interface A B command does the same), and Export CSV saves the interaction table. See Analysis for the rest of the Analysis tab.

Ligand chemistry

Bond orders, aromatic rings and formal charges come from the wwPDB Chemical Component Dictionary:

  • from the file, when its mmCIF carries the dictionary tables (RCSB's files do);
  • from a built-in table for standard residues;
  • otherwise, from one small dictionary file per ligand, downloaded and cached.

A dictionary entry applies only when the ligand's atom names match it. Sticks show double and triple bonds and aromatic rings; Bond orders in the Style tab applies them to ligands, to all residues, or turns them off.

Interactions use the same chemistry for donors, acceptors, aromatic rings and charged groups, with basic groups protonated by rule: in imatinib, only the methylpiperazine nitrogen is charged. For docked ligands, see Ligands and docking.

Labels and chains

  • Label (or L) adds 3D labels to the selected residues. The label and unlabel commands add and remove them.
  • Isolate chain hides the other chains.
  • In the Chains card, click a chain to show or hide it, or double-click it to show only that chain.

Measurements

Pick a ruler in the toolbar, or press D (distance), A (angle) or T (torsion, or dihedral), then click 2, 3 or 4 atoms. Values appear as 3D labels and in the Measurements card. Remove one with ×, or press Backspace to remove the last; Esc clears the ruler.

The distance command measures between two atoms named as atom specs. For example, distance /A:769@N to :AQ4@N2 measures the hinge hydrogen bond to erlotinib in 1M17 (2.70 Å).

Biological assemblies and ensembles

  • Assemblies. When a file defines biological assemblies (PDBx/mmCIF pdbx_struct_assembly or PDB REMARK 350), choose one under Biological assembly in the Structure tab. The assembly command lists them, assembly <id> builds one, and assembly au returns to the asymmetric unit. Assemblies above 300,000 atoms per model are shown as unavailable.
  • Ensembles. Multi-model files, such as NMR ensembles, show a model slider with a play button. Overlay models in the Analysis tab (or the overlay command) superposes every model on the core they share and shows them all at once, with the mean RMSD and per-residue RMSF; color by Ensemble flexibility (RMSF) to see the flexible regions.

Assemblies and superposition. Changing the assembly of a structure undoes its superposition, because assembly operators are defined in the deposited coordinate frame. See Comparing structures.
