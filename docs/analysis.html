Analysis

The per-residue analyses in the Analysis tab: secondary structure, solvent accessibility, part-sphere exposure and disorder, the Ramachandran plot, the per-residue profile, and conservation from a sequence alignment.

The Analysis tab also holds structure comparison, the validation report and density map, interactions, and the predicted aligned error (PAE) of predicted models. Each has its own page: Comparing structures, Validation and density maps, Ligands and docking, and Predictions and AlphaFold for the PAE heatmap, contact probabilities, rigid domains and MSA depth.

Secondary structure

Helices and strands come from one of two sources:

  • Auto uses the file's HELIX/SHEET records or struct_conf annotations when present, and otherwise computes DSSP (Kabsch & Sander 1983).
  • DSSP always recomputes.

Chains with only Cα atoms fall back to an estimate from Cα geometry. The selection card shows each residue's secondary structure and its source, including the DSSP code.

Solvent accessibility

Compute SASA runs the Shrake–Rupley algorithm with a 1.4 Å probe. It gives:

  • the total solvent-accessible surface area of each chain;
  • the relative exposure of each residue, using the maximum ASA values of Tien et al. 2013;
  • each chain's buried surface area in the complex;
  • after an interface analysis (Analyze interface, see Ligands and docking), the buried surface area of that interface.

Once computed, the relative SASA appears in the selection card and the per-residue profile, and you can select by it, for example rsa > 0.4.

Part-sphere exposure (pPSE)

Part-sphere exposure follows StructureMap (Bludau et al. 2022), which was built to put PTM sites in structural context.

  • For each residue, it counts the Cα atoms within 12 Å in a 70° cone along the residue's Cα→Cβ direction.
  • Residues with pPSE ≤ 5 are highly exposed.
  • With a PAE matrix (AlphaFold and other predictions), a neighbor counts only when its distance plus the PAE is within the radius. A confidently placed neighbor counts; a floppy one does not.

Disordered regions

The same count in a 24 Å sphere, smoothed over ±10 residues, marks intrinsically disordered regions. After the pPSE analysis, the idr set selects them.

Color the structure by pPSE, plot it in the per-residue profile, or select with ppse < 6. The proteomics sites table reports each site's pPSE and whether it lies in a disordered region; see Proteomics. From the command line, exposure covers pPSE and disorder.

Ramachandran plot

The plot shows φ/ψ for each residue on the MolProbity Top8000 contours:

  • Residues are favored, allowed (yellow) or outliers (red).
  • Choose a residue category to see its own contours.
  • Glycine and proline are marked.
  • Click a point to select the residue.

For the Ramachandran and side-chain outliers in a PDB entry's wwPDB validation report, see Validation and density maps.

Conservation

Compute conservation scores each residue from a multiple sequence alignment and colors the structure by the result.

The alignment

Use either:

  • the MSA of an opened prediction, or
  • an alignment file whose first sequence is the protein: A3M, aligned FASTA, Stockholm or Clustal.

The score

Choose one of two scores:

  • the Jensen–Shannon divergence of Capra and Singh (2007), with sequence weighting, a gap penalty and a 3-residue window, or
  • Shannon entropy.

Colors and grades

Residues are colored in ConSurf's nine grades, from variable (turquoise) to conserved (maroon). The grades follow the protein's own score distribution. Select with conservation > 0.6 or grade >= 8.

Example: p53 on DNA

For p53 on DNA (1TUP, one of the bundled examples), with a UniRef90 alignment of the DNA-binding domain, the most conserved residues are the zinc ligands C176, H179, C238 and C242. They are followed by S241, R175, and R248 and R273, which contact the DNA.

From the command line, see conservation in Selections and commands.
