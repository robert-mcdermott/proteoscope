Ligands and docking

Where Proteoscope gets a ligand's chemistry, which interactions it finds in a binding site, and how to open, score and compare docking poses in their receptor.

Ligand chemistry

Bond orders, aromatic rings and formal charges come from the wwPDB Chemical Component Dictionary. Proteoscope takes them from one of three places:

  • The file itself, when its mmCIF carries the dictionary tables (_chem_comp_atom and _chem_comp_bond). RCSB's files do.
  • A built-in table for standard residues.
  • Otherwise, one small dictionary file per ligand, downloaded and cached.

A dictionary entry applies only when the ligand's atom names match it. In PDB files, duplicated CONECT records also give bond orders.

Bond orders in the view

Sticks show double and triple bonds and aromatic rings. Choose where they are drawn with Bond orders in the Style tab: ligands, all residues, or off.

Binding-site interactions

Double-click a ligand or residue, or select it and press F, to focus it:

  • The camera frames the binding site.
  • Side chains within 5 Å appear.
  • Non-covalent interactions are detected and drawn as colored dashed lines.

The right panel lists the interactions of the focused residue. For selecting, labels and the rest of the view, see Viewing and interactions.

Interaction types

The criteria follow PLIP (Salentin et al. 2015). Proteoscope detects:

  • hydrogen bonds;
  • salt bridges;
  • π-stacking, parallel and T-shaped;
  • cation-π interactions;
  • hydrophobic contacts;
  • halogen bonds;
  • metal coordination;
  • water bridges, when water is shown (press W).

Each type can be turned on or off in the Analysis tab.

How interactions use the chemistry

Interaction detection uses the ligand chemistry above to find donors, acceptors, aromatic rings and charged groups. Basic groups are protonated by rule: in imatinib, only the methylpiperazine nitrogen is charged.

Chain interfaces and export

  • Analyze interface lists the contacts between two chains. From the command line, use interface A B.
  • Export CSV saves the interaction table.

After an interface analysis, solvent accessibility also reports the buried surface area of that interface; see Analysis.

Opening docking poses

Open or drop an SDF or molfile (V2000 or V3000), MOL2 or PDBQT file (.sdf, .mol, .mol2, .pdbqt) with the receptor active, and its molecules become poses in the receptor. Poses from AutoDock Vina, smina, GNINA, Glide, GOLD, DOCK, rDock and DiffDock come with their scores.

Note. Poses open into the active structure, so open the receptor first.

Scores

Proteoscope reads the scores from:

WhereScores
SD propertiesVina, smina and GNINA affinities and CNN scores; Glide; GOLD; rDock
MOL2 commentsDOCK
PDBQT remarksVina
File namesDiffDock, for example rank1_confidence-0.52.sdf

The pose table shows the main scores. Click a column header to sort by it.

Stepping through poses

Click a pose in the table, press [ and ], or run pose 3. The shown pose is focused with its interactions. The table gives each pose's heavy-atom RMSD to pose 1.

Interaction fingerprints

The fingerprint lists every pose's interactions with the receptor residues around it, as a pose × residue table. Use it to find the poses that keep the interactions you trust, such as a hinge hydrogen bond or a salt bridge.

Export CSV saves the scores and fingerprints.

Poses, crystal ligands and sessions

  • A pose never bonds to a crystal ligand in the same place, so you can compare poses with the deposited ligand.
  • For the deposited ligand itself, the validation report gives its fit to the density (RSCC, RSRZ) and its Mogul geometry outliers; see Validation and density maps.
  • Sessions keep the poses, with the pose files embedded; see Sessions, figures and methods.
