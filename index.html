Explore, check and explain protein structures

Proteoscope is a 3D viewer and analysis workbench for protein and nucleic-acid structures, predicted complexes, density maps and structural proteomics data. It runs on your own computer as one self-contained program, installed with a single command and needing nothing else: no packages, no account. Your files never leave your machine.

Install Proteoscope Read the guide 
$ curl --proto '=https' --tlsv1.2 -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/robert-mcdermott/proteoscope/main/install.sh | sh

Installs the latest release in ~/.local/bin after checking its SHA-256 checksum, then prints how to start it. Other options

PS> irm https://raw.githubusercontent.com/robert-mcdermott/proteoscope/main/install.ps1 | iex

Run in PowerShell. No administrator rights needed. Then open a new terminal and run proteoscope. Other options

Why Proteoscope

Private by design

Files are read in your browser and never uploaded; searches send only what you search for. Only public databases are contacted, and --offline turns even that off.

Self-contained

One program for macOS, Linux and Windows, installed with one command. No Python, packages or other software to set up, and no account or administrator rights.

Checked against the originals

Statistics, alignments, conservation, map fit and Ramachandran classes reproduce limma, MSstatsPTM, US-align, Capra and Singh's scorer, EMDB and MolProbity.

Ready to cite

A methods paragraph with references for what you did, and a citation file for Proteoscope itself.

What it does

The everyday structural biology loop, in one place

Find a structure, style it, see how a ligand binds, judge the model, compare it, and bring in your own data, then export the figure and a methods paragraph to go with it.

Find and open

Every structure and model of your protein

Search by gene, protein name, UniProt accession, keywords or a pasted sequence. The experimental structures are ranked by coverage and resolution, under a track of how many structures cover each residue, next to the models from AlphaFold DB, SWISS-MODEL and other 3D-Beacons providers.

  • Open any of them, or add it superposed on the active structure
  • PDB, PDBx/mmCIF and BinaryCIF, gzip or Zstandard compressed
  • 27 bundled examples that work offline, each with an opening view
Finding structures
Binding sites

See how a ligand binds

Double-click a ligand to frame its binding site, show the side chains around it and draw its interactions with PLIP's criteria: hydrogen bonds, salt bridges, π-stacking, cation–π, hydrophobic, halogen and metal contacts. Bond orders, aromatic rings and charges come from the wwPDB Chemical Component Dictionary.

  • Chain–chain interfaces, with buried surface area
  • Molecular, solvent-accessible and Gaussian surfaces with electrostatics
  • A command line: show sticks within 5 of resn STI
Viewing and interactions
Predicted complexes

Decide which predicted interfaces to believe

Drop an output folder from AlphaFold 3, AlphaFold Server, Boltz, Chai-1, ColabFold, Protenix or OpenFold3. Proteoscope ranks the models by the tool's own score and scores every chain pair from its PAE: ipTM, ipSAE, pDockQ, pDockQ2 and LIS, reproducing Dunbrack's ipsae.py.

  • A PAE plot linked to the 3D view, and rigid domains clustered from it
  • pLDDT, contact probabilities and MSA depth
  • How many of your cross-links each model satisfies
Predictions and AlphaFold
Validation and density

Check the model against the data

Load the wwPDB validation report onto the structure: outliers per residue, clashes drawn in 3D, and the fit to density with percentiles. Show the 2Fo-Fc and Fo-Fc maps of an X-ray entry, the EMDB map of a cryo-EM entry, or your own CCP4 or MRC file.

  • Map fit: atom inclusion as EMDB reports it, and density per residue
  • Ramachandran classes on MolProbity's Top8000 contours, for predicted models too
  • Select problems: outliers and within 5 of ligand
Validation and density maps
Comparison

Compare structures, conformations and predictions

Put several structures in one scene and superpose them by sequence, pruning outliers so a moving domain does not drag the fit, or by structure alone with TM-align and MM-align, ported from US-align. Color by deviation or lDDT to see what moved.

  • RMSD, TM-score and lDDT, overall and per chain
  • One click to compare a structure with its AlphaFold model
  • NMR ensembles overlaid, with per-residue RMSF
Comparing structures
Docking

Triage docking poses

Open the SDF, MOL2 or PDBQT output of AutoDock Vina, smina, GNINA, Glide, GOLD, DOCK, rDock or DiffDock in its receptor. Sort by score, step through the poses, and compare their interaction fingerprints to find the poses that keep the contacts you trust.

  • Scores read from each tool's own output
  • Heavy-atom RMSD of every pose to pose 1
  • A pose × residue fingerprint map, exported as CSV
Ligands and docking
Proteomics

Put your mass spectrometry results on the structure

Open reports from MaxQuant, DIA-NN, Spectronaut, FragPipe, mzTab or Proteome Discoverer; multi-gigabyte reports are streamed, never uploaded. See coverage, localized PTM sites and fold changes on the structure, and test every feature with limma's moderated t-test and MSstatsPTM's adjustment for protein changes.

  • A volcano plot linked to the sites on the structure
  • Cross-links checked by Cα–Cα and solvent-accessible surface distance
  • HDX-MS with the hybrid significance test and Woods plots
Proteomics
Evolution and variants

See what evolution conserves, and which variants matter

Score conservation from an alignment with the Jensen–Shannon divergence of Capra and Singh, in ConSurf's colors. Color human proteins by AlphaMissense pathogenicity, and map variants and PTM sites by structure or UniProt numbering.

  • A prediction's MSA, or A3M, FASTA, Stockholm and Clustal files
  • Part-sphere exposure and disorder, as in StructureMap
  • UniProt domains, sites and disease variants
Analysis
Reproducible

Figures, sessions and methods you can publish

Export images up to 4× with a transparent background, save the whole workspace as a session, share a link that rebuilds the view, or open it in Mol* through MolViewSpec. The Methods button drafts a methods paragraph from what you did, with numbered references and BibTeX.

  • Data sources with their IDs, versions and dates
  • Every analysis with its parameters
  • Scripts from Python or Jupyter with --remote-control
Sessions, figures and methods
Rendering

Figures that look like figures

A WebGPU renderer ray-casts atoms and bonds, adds ambient occlusion, outlines and depth fog, and stays interactive at 100,000 atoms. Clipping planes cut slabs without rebuilding anything.
