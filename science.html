The science behind Proteoscope

Proteoscope reimplements published methods rather than inventing its own. This page lists them with their references, shows how Proteoscope's results are checked against the tools the methods come from, and explains how to cite Proteoscope.

How the analyses are checked

A validation suite in the repository, validation/, recomputes numbers produced by the reference implementations and compares them with Proteoscope's. The reference tools are not part of Proteoscope; only their outputs are stored. Continuous integration runs the offline suites on every change and all of them weekly.

Other checks were run while each feature was built:

  • Interface scores reproduce Dunbrack's ipsae.py (version 4) to its printed precision on the AlphaFold 3 (Aurora A–TPX2) and AlphaFold 2 multimer (RAF1–KSR1–MEK1) examples of the IPSAE repository and on a Boltz-2 prediction.
  • TM-align matches US-align on 606 all-against-all pairs of 25 protein chains and 3 RNAs, and on edge cases from 3-residue chains to the 959-residue SARS-CoV-2 spike.
  • Superposition gives the expected results on known cases: adenylate kinase closed onto open, 1.08 Å over 112 core pairs; SUMO-1 onto ubiquitin by structure alone, TM-score 0.654, as US-align gives.
  • Conservation of p53 from 1,384 sequences ranks the zinc ligands C176, H179, C238 and C242 first, then S241, R175, R248 and R273.

The validation README has every detail, including where Proteoscope and a reference tool differ and why.

Data sources and credits

  • Structures, validation reports and density maps come from the Protein Data Bank and EMDB archives (wwPDB, CC0), through RCSB PDB and PDBe.
  • Predicted models and AlphaMissense predictions come from AlphaFold DB (CC BY 4.0; Jumper et al., Nature 2021; Varadi et al., Nucleic Acids Research 2024; Cheng et al., Science 2023).
  • The MolProbity Top8000 Ramachandran distributions come from the Richardson Lab's reference_data (CC BY 4.0; Williams et al., Protein Science 2018).
  • Ligand chemistry comes from the wwPDB Chemical Component Dictionary (Westbrook et al., Bioinformatics 2015; CC0).
  • Searches use RCSB PDB, UniProt, PDBe and 3D-Beacons (Varadi et al., GigaScience 2022); models downloaded from a 3D-Beacons provider carry that provider's terms.
  • Public proteomics evidence comes from the EBI Proteins API (Nightingale et al., Nucleic Acids Research 2017), which collects PeptideAtlas, ProteomicsDB, PRIDE and PTMeXchange data.
  • The bundled examples are PDB entries and the AlphaFold DB model of p53 with its PAE (AF-P04637-F1); each example's citation is shown with it.
  • Screenshots on this site use public data: PDB entries, the AlphaFold Server example of the IPSAE repository, AutoDock Vina's tutorial poses, and the example data of MSstatsPTM, xiVIEW and HaDeX.
